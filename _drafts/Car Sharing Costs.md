Frisbee tournaments are often over 100km away and most people come by driving, given the long distance it is cost effective to carpool. Typically in clubs a self nominated driver takes passangers, and the passengers reimburse fuel costs.

This seems fair, however it significantly underpays the driver, who not only incurrs wear and tear, but is also providing a service by driving. 
This paper shows alternative modes of transport, how expensive they are and how much less ocnvinienct they are.
In other words the passengers are getting an excellent service, very cheaply and that the driver is paying for it. 

Consider the costs for going from Sydney to Canberra (300km) and Sydney to Shepparton (700km).

Uber
This is absurdly expensive, however this example is the closest alternative which replicates the convinence offered by driving and the effort put forth by the driver driving their car the whole distance to the tournament and it hightlights the costs incurred by the driver.

Sydney to Shepparton: Cost $2,150 Time: 15 Hrs Convinience:10

The ATO gives a deduction of $0.78 per km, this is to cover all types of cars. I'm assuming frisbee players are a little more efficient and place an expense of $0.60 per km. This covers the wear and tear and fuel costs, it doesn't cover the service of driving (which can be shared). $0.60 per km is easy to split in to 2,3,4,5 and 6  (.30, .20, .15, .12, .10 $ per km ) depending on the number of passengers. 

This means sydney to canberra, (approximately 600km return) would cost $360, a 4 person car pool means $90 each. An equivalent bus ride would cost $80, only take you to the city centre (or airport) which is less convinient.

Sydney to Shepparton is 1400km return , a 4 person carpool means each person pays $210 each
