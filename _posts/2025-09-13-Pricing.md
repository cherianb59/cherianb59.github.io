## Pricing - Value vs Cost and Rent Seeking

Prices affect nearly every decision people make, from what food they buy to where they live. Understanding why goods and services cost what they do requires looking beyond production costs and examining how markets, competition, and rules shape prices.

### Cost Pricing 

Cost-based pricing sets prices by adding a margin on top of production costs.

These costs include:

- Raw materials

- Labour

- Rent and utilities

- Equipment and maintenance

Under this approach:

- Price is closely linked to cost

- Profit margins are usually stable

- Competition tends to push prices down

Cost-based pricing is common in industries with:

- Many suppliers

- Standardised products

- Low barriers to entry

### Value Pricing 

Characteristics:

- Prices can be much higher than production costs

- Different customers may pay different prices

- Branding and convenience matter

Examples:

- Software subscriptions

- Medical services

- Education

- Entertainment

Value-based pricing becomes more common when:

- Alternatives are limited

- The product is differentiated

- Switching costs are high

### Commodities 

Commodities are goods that are largely identical regardless of who produces them.

Key features:

- Standardised quality

- Many sellers

- Prices set by the market, not the producer

Examples:

- Wheat

- Oil

- Iron ore

- Electricity (at wholesale level)

Because commodities are interchangeable, sellers have little control over pricing.

The opposite of a commodity is a differentiated product.

#### Differentiated products:

- Vary in quality, branding, or features

- Allow producers to charge different prices

- Low competition

Examples:

- Branded clothing

- Smartphones

- Professional services

- Patented medicines

Differentiation gives sellers more pricing power.

### Rent Seeking 

Barriers to entry are obstacles that make it difficult for new competitors to enter a market.

Common barriers include:

- Licensing requirements

- Long training periods

- Legal restrictions

- High startup costs

Barriers reduce competition and increase prices.

#### Examples

##### Actuaries

- Long qualification process

- Limited number of certified professionals

- High salaries supported by restricted supply

##### Tradies

- Licensing and certification requirements

- Regulation varies by region

- Limits competition in local markets

##### Doctors and Dentists

- Controlled training places

- Professional accreditation

- Restrictions on who can practice

##### Migration

- Skilled migration limits labour supply

- Can raise wages in protected professions

#### Regulatory Capture

- Industries influence regulators

- Rules benefit existing firms

- New entrants face higher compliance costs

### Price Discrimination

Price discrimination occurs when the same product is sold at different prices to different customers, without cost differences explaining the price change.

#### Hardback vs Paperback Books

- Content is identical

- Hardbacks are released first at higher prices and sold at a higher price to capture the people who want the book right now

- Paperback editions are cheaper and target more price-sensitive readers

Publishers capture more revenue from customers willing to pay more.

#### Student, Adult, and Senior Pricing

Common examples include:

- Public transport

- Movies

- Museums

Lower prices are offered to students and seniors, this increases total sales while maintaining higher prices for others.
