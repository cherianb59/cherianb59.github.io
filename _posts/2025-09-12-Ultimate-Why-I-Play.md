## Ultimate Frisbee - Why I play

I've been playing Ultimate for seven years now, and I probably spend way too much time thinking about it. I'm always trying to convince friends and colleagues to come out and play, and I usually find myself rattling off the same list of  reasons. But the real reason I'm still playing isn't on that list.

### The Easy Answers

If someone asks why they should try Ultimate, I give them the simple, practical answers.

First, throwing a disc is just plain fun. There's a unique satisfaction in it, just like the pure joy of kicking a soccer ball perfectly or slogging a cricket ball with the meat of the bat. Learning to control the disc, making it curve around a defender, float perfectly to a receiver, or cut through the wind is very rewarding to master.

It's also a very cheap sport. To get a game going, you just need a $20 disc, eight cones to mark a field, and some grass or sand. Compare that to sports like lacrosse or ice hockey, which require hundreds of dollars in specialised equipment just to get started (I don't understand how these sports are more popular than ultimate). Like soccer, the barrier to entry is almost zero, which keeps the sport grounded and open to everyone.

Tied to that, the sport is self-refereed. Having no referees helps keeps tournament games cheap. It also builds an anti dickhead culture, if you're a dickhead people won't accept your calls and just contest everything, also your spirit captain should (in theory) try and rectify you .

### The Real Reason

I've told myself for years that those are the reasons I play. They make sense, they're easy to explain, and they're all true. But they aren't the real reason.

The real reason I play, and why most people stick with their sports teams, work teams, or any group for that matter, is the people.

Looking back, the experience of showing up every week to run around and play with your friends is what got me truly addicted to the sport. It's the shared struggle of playing a tough team, the off field banter, the sideline throws. You build a unique bond when you compete together.

That community is what lasts. Even after I left Canberra in 2022, those connections didn't just disappear. I still catch up with my old teammates whenever they're in Sydney or I'm back in their city. These aren't just people I used to play a sport with; they're lifelong friends. That's why I started playing, and that's why I'll keep playing.
