The best propaganda is the one you don't even notice. 

Communist dictatorships need military parades, giant statues and simplistic posters. Australia just needs a bugle at 5:00 AM and some biscuits. The second you question the myth, you’re accused of disrespecting war heroes.

It’s the most brilliant con in Australian history. Here’s why:

1. We lost. Gallipoli wasn’t a triumph. It was a catastrophic military cock-up. Over 8,700 Australians died in eight months, achieved zero strategic goals, and then snuck away at midnight. The Turks were the ones defending their homeland from foreign invaders. Somehow, a bloody defeat orchestrated by incompetent British generals got repackaged as our spiritual birthday.

2. We were imperial lackeys, not an independent nation. Calling Gallipoli the "birth of the nation" is pure BS. We weren’t fighting for Australian freedom, we were cannon fodder for Winston Churchill’s botched naval gamble. We shipped 15,000 kilometres to invade a country that posed zero threat to us simply because London called.

3. It covers up the wars at home. Why does our national warrior myth start in Turkey in 1915? Because admitting where our real fighting happened means talking about the Frontier Wars. It’s far more comfortable to mourn white diggers dying overseas than to acknowledge the massacres and land theft at home. Gallipoli gave white Australia a clean, guilt-free origin story.

4. It’s a blank cheque for future wars. The myth has been weaponised into "Anzackery." Politicians wrap themselves in the slouch hat, weapons manufacturers sponsor exhibits at the Australian War Memorial, and military spending gets put beyond reproach. By turning war into a sacred tragedy rather than a political failure, it conditions us never to question why we keep tagging along to the next disaster, from Vietnam to Iraq and Afghanistan.

Mourning the terrified kids slaughtered in the trenches is human. But turning an imperial slaughter into an untouchable national religion is propaganda.

The greatest trick of ANZAC Day is making you repeat "Lest We Forget," while ensuring you forget the only question that actually matters. Why the hell were they there in the first place?
