## Why Veganism Is Morally Right 

I’m not vegan, and I probably never will be. It’s a strange way to begin a post arguing for veganism, but it’s the truth. I eat animal products because, frankly, I enjoy the taste. But this is a weakness, not a justification. I'm trying to reduce my consumption, but this doesn't change that in today's world, for those of us with choices, veganism is morally superior. Meat eaters (or carnists as vegans call them) should accept that what they are doing is wrong instead of being so defensive.

The first and most brutal truth is the systemic cruelty inherent in modern animal agriculture. The journey of meat, dairy, and eggs from a living creature to a supermarket product is a horror show. Animals are not "raised"; they are produced.

- Pigs, who are smarter than dogs, are often kept in gestation crates so small they can't even turn around.

- Male chicks in the egg industry are deemed useless, so they are thrown into grinders alive or gassed on their first day of life.

- Dairy cows are forcibly impregnated year after year, and their calves are stolen from them shortly after birth so we can drink their milk. When their bodies are exhausted, they are sent to slaughter.

This isn't an aberration; this is the standard, accepted practice. These industries treat sentient, feeling beings as mere cogs in a machine, units of production whose suffering is factored in as a cost of doing business.

Also I dont think honey is bad, bees are too dumb to suffer. 

### Meat is not Necessary Anymore 

A common defense of meat-eating is that "we've always done it." For most of human history, and for some people in remote areas today, hunting and animal husbandry were essential for survival. But for the vast majority of people reading this in a developed country, that excuse has expired.

We are not cavemen huddling around a fire. We live in an age of nutritional science and global supply chains. We have access to fortified plant milks, a vast array of legumes, grains, and vegetables, and meat alternatives that get more realistic every year. Eating animals is no longer about survival it's about habit, convenience, and pleasure. When survival is not on the line, can we really justify the immense suffering and death we cause for a fleeting sensory experience?


### The Moral Comparison: Carnism and Slavery

Today's carnists are the ideological descendants of slaveowners.

This analogy is not meant to equate the suffering of animals with the horrors of human slavery but it points to the same underlying structure of thought: that it is acceptable to treat a sentient being as property, to exploit its body, and to take its life for one's own benefit. Slaveowners justified their actions by dehumanizing their victims, claiming they were inferior or lacked the capacity for complex thought and emotion. Today, carnism operates on a similar principle called speciesism, an arbitrary prejudice based on species membership. We have simply drawn the line of moral consideration around our own species, a line that is intellectually and ethically baseless.

### If Eating Meat Is Okay, Why Not Eat Dogs and Cats?

One hypocriscy of fellow carnists that annoys me is they think eating dogs, cats and horses is bad, but other animals are okay. Why? What is the morally relevant difference between a pig and a dog?

If you argue that dogs are more intelligent, you're likely wrong—pigs consistently outperform them in cognitive tests. If you say it's because we've welcomed dogs into our homes as companions, you're pointing to a human cultural practice, not an inherent quality in the animal. There is no consistent trait that farm animals lack that our pets possess. We lavish affection on some animals while subjecting others, who are just as capable of feeling pain, fear, and joy, to cruelty. The only difference is our perception. If it’s morally acceptable to eat a cow, it must also be acceptable to eat a dog. If you can’t accept the latter, you must question the former.

### Quantifying Animal Consumption Impact

One point that often gets missed in vegan arguments is that some meats have a far bigger environmental cost than others. For example beef and lamb require huge amounts of land, water, and feed, and they produce far more greenhouse gases than pork or chicken. So while all meat involves suffering, some choices are much worse for the planet than others.

This logic also applies to diary and eggs. If it takes roughly 10 liters of milk to produce 1 kilogram of hard cheese, then the environmental impact and animal suffering involved are magnified. 

For this reason, consuming a kilogram of cheese can be significantly worse than consuming a liter of milk or even some kinds of meat. It’s a stark reminder that simply cutting out meat isn’t a complete ethical solution if it’s replaced with a massive increase in dairy consumption.

Quantifying CO2 damage is fairly simple, if a cow produces 800 tonnes of CO2 over its lifetime, and 400kg of meat, the consuming 1kg contrinutes 2 tonnes of CO2.
